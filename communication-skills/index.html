📖 Read the passage and answer Questions 23–34 (Emeka & Victor)

No one would believe that Emeka and Victor were twins; they were totally different from each other. Although Emeka was only ten minutes older than his twin brother, relatives seemed ridiculously obsessed with this sheer accident of their birth. Mother, father, aunties and uncles regularly drummed the point into Victor's head. They told him, in no uncertain terms, that, in the patrilineal set-up of their society, he had to succumb to the whims and caprices of Emeka, because the latter was the first-born son of the family. Everyone wondered how it was that Victor could be calmness personified, while his twin brother was as boisterous as he was aggressive. Their classmates concealed their regard for Victor in their unlikely taunt, "Young old man".

When their mother was going away for a fortnight, she called all four of her children and told them that she had to leave everything in Emeka's hands. He let hell loose in the form of rowdy parties, eating sprees, video sessions… the complete works! When, by Saturday of the first week, the children found the food cupboard totally empty, because its contents had been gobbled down by Emeka's gang, Victor realized it was time for him to do something. Without making any fuss, he took his sisters to their aunt's house to eat once a day. The girls were never really full, but Victor convinced them that one always made sacrifices and bore discomfort bravely when one's mother was away. He could not bear was Victor's silent scorn and utter contempt for him. "Damn Victor," he swore vulgarly, and wished he could infect this paragon with his wild ways.

As soon as their mother returned, the girls launched into an elaborate account of conditions under Emeka's "regime". Just as the girls finished complaining, Emeka sauntered into the house. Nobody said anything to him. In fact, you could hear a pin drop. The silence was far worse than a slap would have been.
